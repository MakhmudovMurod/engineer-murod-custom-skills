# Uzbek Telegram Native-Ad Copy

## Purpose

Create ads that read like a useful, witty Uzbek Telegram post—not translated corporate copy. The method comes from a public-corpus analysis of 1,666 accessible posts across `@trolluz`, `@teacherazam`, `@kunduziy`, `@ziyokhonov`, and `@gafurovumid`, with close review of native integrations, direct ads, campaign bridges, CTAs, and disclosure patterns.

Use broad techniques learned across multiple channels. Never reproduce a source post or claim to write exactly as a living author.

## Default Output Contract

Unless the user requests otherwise:

1. Write in natural Uzbek Latin script.
2. Return the strongest ready-to-publish ad first, without a long preamble.
3. Use one clear CTA and one destination link placeholder if no link was supplied.
4. Keep unverifiable claims out. Never invent customer counts, discounts, deadlines, prices, personal use, or results.
5. Label paid promotion transparently with `Reklama` / `Hamkorlik asosida` or the user's required disclosure.
6. If essential facts are missing, ask only for facts that change the promise: product, audience, offer, proof, CTA/link, restrictions. For a draft request, use visible placeholders instead of stalling.
7. Do not automatically add Russian translation. Add it only when requested or when the audience brief requires bilingual copy.

## First Decision: Pick the Right Ad Mode

Choose one mode before drafting.

### A. Problem → recognition → relief (default)

Best for apps, services, fintech, insurance, marketplaces, education, and practical products.

Structure:

1. A familiar local frustration.
2. A scene the reader recognizes.
3. A short pivot: `Xullas, ...`
4. Product as a believable solution.
5. 2–4 concrete benefits in everyday situations.
6. Proof, price, condition, or caveat.
7. One low-friction CTA.

### B. First-person test or mini-story

Use only when the user provides a real experience or the advertiser authorizes a truthful test.

Structure: need → search/attempt → what happened → why it mattered → who it fits → CTA.

Never fabricate `sinab ko‘rdim`, `sotib oldim`, `bordim`, or a family story.

### C. Context bridge

Connect the product to a current or preceding channel topic: travel → tickets, road safety → insurance/taxi safety, study season → university, holiday → gift/offer.

The bridge must be real, immediate, and understandable in one sentence. Do not force a trending event onto an unrelated product.

### D. Self-aware / meta-humor

Acknowledge that the audience expects an ad, then use the expectation as the joke. Effective for creator-led channels with established rapport.

Use sparingly. The joke should reveal the offer, not hide weak product value. Never use humor to obscure sponsorship.

### E. Direct utility card

Best for deadlines, events, ticket sales, admissions, exact prices, schedules, or limited inventory.

Format: strongest outcome → key facts → exact conditions → deadline/scarcity → CTA. This is less native but clearer when details matter more than personality.

## The Core Native Formula: TANISH

Use **TANISH** as the drafting scaffold:

- **T — Tanish vaziyat:** Start with a recognizable moment, tension, question, or contrarian line.
- **A — Aniqlik:** Name the pain concretely in the reader's world.
- **N — Natija:** State the useful outcome before drowning the reader in features.
- **I — Isbot:** Add a demo, mechanism, real number, condition, or truthful personal evidence.
- **S — Soddalik:** Translate features into scenes and plain Uzbek.
- **H — Harakat:** End with one clear, low-friction action.

A strong post usually feels like: **gap → muammo → yechim → dalil → harakat**, not **brand → adjectives → feature dump**.

## Natural Uzbek Voice Rules

### Sound spoken, not translated

Prefer:

- `Tanish holat-a?`
- `Xullas, ...`
- `Aytgancha, ...`
- `Qarang, ...`
- `O‘zi ...`
- `Mabodo ...`
- `Shunaqa paytda ...`
- `Eng yaxshi joyi — ...`
- `Gap shundaki, ...`
- `Sizga kerak bo‘ladigani — ...`

These are a palette, not mandatory catchphrases. Use at most one or two per post.

Prefer concrete verbs and reader scenes over abstract nouns:

- Weak: `Xizmat foydalanuvchi tajribasini optimallashtiradi.`
- Better: `Navbatda turmaysiz: ilovadan vaqtni tanlaysiz, mutaxassis keladi.`

- Weak: `Innovatsion moliyaviy yechim.`
- Better: `Kim qancha va qachon qaytarishini botning o‘zi eslatib turadi.`

### Rhythm

- Open with 1–2 short lines.
- Let the middle breathe with short paragraphs or 3–5 bullets.
- Mix short punches with medium explanatory sentences.
- One thought per paragraph.
- Use dashes for a spoken aside, not in every sentence.
- Use questions only when they create recognition; do not stack rhetorical questions.

### Register

- Default: respectful conversational `siz`.
- Use `do‘stlar`, `o‘rtoqlar`, `akalar` only if the channel's real voice supports it.
- Light Russian/English code-switching (`bot`, `startap`, `online`) is acceptable when it is normal for the audience. Do not sprinkle slang for effect.
- Keep useful Uzbek imperfections, but do not intentionally add misspellings.
- Avoid bureaucratic words (`mazkur`, `amalga oshiriladi`, `taqdim etiladi`, `foydalanishingiz mumkin`) when a direct verb works.

## Hook Library

Pick one relevant mechanism; do not combine several.

1. **Pain inversion:** `Do‘stingizni yo‘qotmoqchimisiz? Unga qarz bering.`
2. **Recognition:** `Tanish holat-a? Kerakli ustani topguncha ishning o‘zi esdan chiqadi.`
3. **Contrarian honesty:** `Odatda “AI bor” degani narxga yana bir nol qo‘shildi degani. Bu safar foydasi ko‘rinib turibdi.`
4. **Personal discovery:** `Bir oy kutaman deb yurgandim. Kerakli narsani o‘zimizdan topdim.`
5. **Timely bridge:** `Aytgancha, yo‘llardagi xavfsizlik haqida...`
6. **Outcome first:** `Inglizchani tushunib, gapira olmay yurganlar uchun 3 kunlik amaliy dars.`
7. **Self-aware:** `Izohlarda “hozir reklama chiqadi” deyapsizlar. Mayli, sizga hech narsa aytmayman...`
8. **Specific utility:** `5 daqiqa, bitta so‘rovnoma, 7 ta sovg‘a.`

Do not copy the sample wording blindly. Adapt the underlying mechanism.

## Turning Features into Uzbek-Life Benefits

For every feature, answer: **`Bu odamning hayotida nimani osonlashtiradi?`**

Use this conversion:

`Feature → everyday scene → practical payoff`

Examples:

- Hidden phone numbers → driver and passenger do not see personal numbers → less privacy anxiety.
- Automatic reminder → user does not repeatedly ask a friend for repayment → relationship friction moves to the bot.
- Image stabilization → a moving child is still sharp → fewer missed family moments.
- Pickup point delivery → no month-long overseas wait → inspect and receive nearby.

Prefer 2–4 vivid benefits to 10 generic features.

## Proof and Credibility

Use the strongest available proof type:

1. Truthful first-hand test.
2. Demonstrable mechanism.
3. Exact terms: price, date, duration, eligibility.
4. Verified result or statistic with source.
5. Transparent limitation or caveat.
6. Social proof supplied by the client and verifiable.

Specificity beats hype. `5 daqiqada`, `22-sentabr 20:00`, `0% — 52 oy`, or a concrete workflow is stronger than `juda qulay`, provided the fact is verified.

For regulated offers—finance, crypto, health, education grants, insurance—state conditions plainly and avoid guaranteed outcomes.

## CTA Rules

- One primary action per post.
- Match the CTA to reader readiness.
- Low-friction native CTAs: `Ko‘rib chiqing`, `Shartlarini tekshiring`, `Sinab ko‘ring`, `Joyni band qiling`, `Ro‘yxatdan o‘ting`.
- Use urgency only when a real deadline, stock limit, or price period exists.
- Put the link in the action phrase, not as a random naked URL, unless Telegram formatting constraints require it.
- Avoid `Hoziroq!!!`, repeated arrows, and several destinations competing at once.

## Disclosure and Trust

Native does not mean disguised.

- Preferred: `Reklama`, `Hamkorlik asosida`, or a platform/client-approved disclosure.
- Place disclosure where it is visible without ruining the opening—usually at the end, or at the start when legally required.
- Never invent a playful disclosure that could mislead readers.
- If the channel has a recurring disclosure phrase, use it only with the owner's approval and preserve legal clarity.

## What Underperforms

Avoid these patterns unless the user explicitly requests a direct corporate announcement:

- Starting with flags, sirens, and the brand name before the reader has a reason to care.
- Translating Russian/English corporate copy word-for-word.
- Feature dumps with no lived scene.
- Unsupported superlatives: `eng zo‘r`, `yagona`, `inqilobiy`, `100% ishonchli`.
- Fake scarcity or a made-up countdown.
- 8–15 emojis, ALL CAPS, decorative symbols, and repeated exclamation marks.
- Three CTAs, three links, and three audiences in one post.
- A personal testimonial the author never experienced.
- Formal phrases like `hurmatli foydalanuvchilar diqqatiga` in creator channels.
- Humor unrelated to the offer.
- Hiding the ad so aggressively that trust is lost.

## Drafting Workflow

1. Extract a mini-brief: product, audience, one pain, one promise, proof, terms, CTA, link, disclosure, channel persona.
2. Identify the **single strongest angle**.
3. Choose one ad mode.
4. Write 5 private hooks; keep the most natural, not the loudest.
5. Draft with TANISH.
6. Replace every abstract feature with a scene or practical consequence.
7. Remove the first paragraph if it is generic throat-clearing.
8. Cut 20% of the words.
9. Verify every number, claim, deadline, personal statement, and link against the brief.
10. Run the quality score below.
11. Return ready-to-publish copy first. Add variants only if requested or genuinely useful.

## Quality Score (100)

Do not present a draft below 80 without warning.

- **Audience recognition — 20:** Does the opening feel immediately familiar?
- **Natural Uzbek — 20:** Spoken rhythm, local phrasing, no translation smell?
- **Value clarity — 15:** Can the reader explain the benefit after one read?
- **Specific proof — 15:** Is there a real mechanism, fact, term, or truthful test?
- **Native fit — 10:** Does it belong in this channel rather than a brochure?
- **CTA focus — 10:** One clear next step with low friction?
- **Trust — 10:** Disclosure, conditions, and claims are honest?

Automatic deductions:

- −20 invented personal story or result
- −15 unsupported superlative/guarantee
- −10 fake urgency
- −10 more than one competing CTA
- −10 corporate translation smell
- −5 excessive emoji or punctuation

## Output Formats

### Standard request

Return:

1. `Tayyor matn` — final publishable copy.
2. `Muqobil sarlavhalar` — two alternative hooks only when useful.
3. `Tekshirish kerak` — factual placeholders or compliance points, if any.

### Fast request (`tez`, `faqat matn`)

Return only the final copy.

### Campaign request

Produce a three-part sequence:

1. **Context/value post** — genuine topic or pain, no forced CTA.
2. **Native integration** — bridge context to the product and proof.
3. **Reminder** — deadline/benefit/FAQ, shorter and more direct.

Never manufacture a fake setup story merely to make the second post look organic.

## Synthetic Template

```text
[Short familiar or contrarian hook]

[Tell the recognizable situation in 2–4 sentences.]

Xullas, [product/service] shu muammoni [plain mechanism] orqali hal qiladi.

— [benefit in an everyday scene]
— [benefit in an everyday scene]
— [verified proof/condition]

[Truthful caveat, price, deadline, or eligibility if relevant.]

[One CTA]: [LINK]

Hamkorlik asosida
```

This is a scaffold, not a fill-in-the-blanks prison. If the result sounds templated, rewrite it.
